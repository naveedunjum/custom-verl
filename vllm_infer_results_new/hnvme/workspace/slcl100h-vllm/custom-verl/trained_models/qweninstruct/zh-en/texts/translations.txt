GB 18030-2022
4-Character Vocabulary
4.1 Overview
The characters included in this document are encoded in single-byte, double-byte, or four-byte formats.
The single-byte section includes all 128 characters (from 0x00 to 0x7F) specified in GB/T 11383-1989.
4.3 Two-byte section
The double-byte section includes all graphic characters from GB/T 2312-1980, CJK Unified Han characters from GB/T 13000, as well as some additional graphic characters.
The two-byte character section shall be handled according to the specifications in Appendix A.
Among these, the graphic form, code position, and function of the ideographic character descriptor shall comply with the specifications in Appendix B.
Note: GB/T 13000 provides unified encoding for Chinese characters used in China, Japan, Korea, Vietnam, and other countries and regions.
Chinese characters with unique abstract glyph forms are assigned individual code positions; Chinese characters from different sources but with the same abstract glyph form are assigned shared code positions.
The encoded Chinese characters are called CJK Unified Ideographs, where CJK stands for China, Japan, and Korea.
4.4 Four-byte portion
The four-byte section includes 66 CJK Unified Ideographs (from 9FA6 to 9FEF, excluding the 8 characters from 9FB4 to 9FBB) specified in GB/T 13000, as well as CJK Unified Ideographs Extension A, B, C, D, and E, and characters of ethnic minority scripts that have already been encoded in GB/T 13000.
The characters in the four-byte portion shall be governed by the provisions in Appendix C.
5 Overall Structure
In the text, if a number is prefixed with "0x", it indicates a hexadecimal representation; if not prefixed with "0x", it indicates a decimal representation.
In the appendix, all encoded representations are expressed in hexadecimal, while other numerical values are expressed in decimal.
The single-byte portion adopts the encoding structure defined in GB/T 11383-1989, using code positions from 0x00 to 0x7F.
The double-byte GB section uses two eight-bit binary byte sequences to represent a character. The first byte ranges from 0x81 to 0xFE, and the second byte ranges from 0x40 to 0x7E or from 0x80 to 0xFE.
The four-byte portion uses the range 0x30 to 0x39 (as defined in GB/11383-1989) as a suffix for extending the double-byte encoding, with a coding range of 0x81308130 to 0xFE39FE39.
The encoding range for the first byte of a four-byte character is 0x81 to 0xFE; the second byte's encoding range is 0x30 to 0x39; the third byte's encoding range is 0x81 to 0xFE; and the fourth byte's encoding range is 0x30 to 0x39. That is: 0x81308130 to 0x81308139; 0x81308230 to 0x81308239; 0x8130FEFE30 to 0x8130FE39; 0x81318130 to 0x81318139; ... 0xFE39FE30 to 0xFE39FE39.

This document specifies three implementation levels.

Achieve level 1 implementation of the single-byte encoding section, the double-byte encoding section, and the four-byte encoding section of this document for CJK Unified Ideographs (i.e., 0x82358F33 to 0x82359636) and CJK Unified Ideographs Extension A (i.e., 0x8139EE39 to 0x82358738).
Any product covered by this document shall meet the requirements for achieving level 1.
Note: According to software application requirements, for level 1 implementation, any one or multiple non-Chinese character scripts listed in Table 3 may be selected.
9.3 Level 2 Implementation
Implementation level 2 includes implementation level 1.
In addition, level 2 implementation also supports the encoded Chinese characters in the "General Standard Chinese Characters Table" that are not included in level 1.
The code positions and character forms of the Chinese characters included in the "General Standard Chinese Characters Table" are listed in the appendix below.
System software and supporting software should meet the requirements for level 2 implementation.
Note: System software and supporting software include but are not limited to operating systems, database management systems, and middleware (information on software product classification is referenced in GB/T 36475).
9.4 Level 3 Implementation
Implementation level 3 includes implementation level 2.
In addition, achieving level 3 shall also support all Chinese characters specified in this document and the radicals listed in Table 3.
Products used in government services and public services should meet the requirements for level 3.
Note: The government services and public service sectors include but are not limited to railway transport, road transport, waterway transport, air transport, multimodal transport and transportation agency services, postal services, financial services, insurance services, land management services, health services, state institutions, and social security (industry classification information can be found in GB/T 4754).

Restrictive measures aimed at preventing the spread of COVID-19 are likely to further squeeze drug traffickers who rely on precursor chemicals.

The most destructive earthquakes in the world over the past 60 years
Chilean earthquake (1960)
At 3:11 p.m. local time on May 22, 1960, the largest earthquake ever recorded in human history occurred in Valdivia, Chile. The moment magnitude of the earthquake was 9.5.
Within just ten minutes, the entire city of Valdivia turned into ruins.
The massive tsunami triggered by the earthquake struck the coast of Chile, surging with waves reaching a height of 25 meters.
The tsunami affected distant Japan and the Philippines.
The number of casualties and economic losses caused by such a widespread disaster cannot be precisely determined.

Alaska Earthquake (1964)
March 27, 1964 was a Friday, and it was also Good Friday.
On that day, a massive earthquake occurred in Alaska, USA, the largest in North American history, with a moment magnitude of 9.2.
Until 2006, the Alaska earthquake was still the third-largest earthquake ever recorded in the world.
When the earthquake occurred, a fault between the Pacific Plate and the North American Plate broke near Prince William Sound, close to the Strait of Fairbanks.
In most areas, the earthquake lasted between 3 and 5 minutes.
The sea bed displacement triggered a massive tsunami with a height of 67 meters.
Since it was a holiday that day, schools and many businesses were closed.
This great earthquake ultimately resulted in 139 deaths.
Of these, 9 people died directly from the earthquake, while the other 130 died from the tsunami caused by the earthquake.

The Tangshan Earthquake (1976)
On July 28, 1976, two strong earthquakes of magnitude 7 or higher occurred in Tangshan and Fengnan County, Hebei Province, China, within a 16-hour period.
The focus is 23 kilometers below the ground.
The entire city of Tangshan experienced complete disruptions in transportation, communication, water supply, and power supply, and was instantly reduced to rubble.
People in Beijing, 140 kilometers away from the epicenter, also felt strong shaking.
According to official statistics, including Beijing and Tianjin, all areas affected by the earthquake suffered 242,769 deaths and 164,851 serious injuries.
The Tangshan earthquake has become China's second deadliest earthquake on record, ranking second only to the 1556 Jianjing earthquake.
However, some overseas scientists estimate that this earthquake could result in at least 650,000 deaths.

NEWS GOV-MO: Police Appeal: The Police Force urges the public to be vigilant against fraudulent short messages from fake telecommunications companies
Recently, the Police Judicial Police Bureau has received multiple reports of telecom fraud cases. Unscrupulous criminals have impersonated local telecommunications companies and sent fraudulent SMS messages to victims, containing links to fake websites. They lure victims into logging into these fake websites to exchange points by falsely claiming that their points are about to expire and must be promptly redeemed for rewards.
The victim entered the credit card details and verification code as instructed. Immediately afterward, they received a notification from the financial institution stating that there were unauthorized consumption records on their credit card, and thus suspected that their credit card information had been stolen through the aforementioned website.
Upon investigation, it was confirmed that the relevant telecommunications companies did not issue the aforementioned short messages.
The Police Force urges the public to remain vigilant if they receive similar SMS messages. Do not visit the website links provided in the messages or disclose any personal information. If asked to provide credit card details, account transaction passwords, or transaction verification codes, you must be especially cautious, as this could prevent credit card fraud.
Anti-fraud reminder:
1. Pay more attention to cybersecurity news and enhance personal awareness of prevention;
2. Do not casually log in to websites of unknown origin or register your personal and credit card information on them, as this could lead to your information being stolen or misused.
3. Be alert to fake and phishing websites, and always pay attention to whether the website URL matches exactly with that of the official website;
4. Install antivirus software on mobile phones, portable devices, or computers, and update it regularly;
5. If you suspect you have encountered fraud or other crimes, you should immediately call the police fraud prevention inquiry hotline at 8800 7777 or the reporting hotline at 993 for assistance.
